Master's thesis, in progress

Graph-based detection of underexplored research areas

The questionCan the shape of the citation graph alone, without reading a single abstract, point to research areas that are about to grow more than expected?

The answer so farYes, modestly. Regions that are coherent, loosely connected inside and cite heavily outward were about 1.6 times as likely as chance to grow above expectation, on 2021 to 2024 data the score never saw while it was being designed.

What it becomesA search tool: type a topic, get a ranked list of nearby regions with the right shape and the papers in each, as a starting point for a researcher choosing what to read.

Whiskers show 95% intervals over 30 clustering seeds. Growth is measured after controlling for region size and citation count, so the score can't win just by picking big, popular areas.

  1. Collect

    527,036 computer-science papers from arXiv, dated to the month.

  2. Repair the graph

    About 75% of each paper's references were missing from the benchmark. Semantic Scholar recovered 95% of known links and added 297,486 new ones.

  3. Embed and cluster

    Papers embedded by their text and, separately, by citation structure alone, then grouped into topic regions.

  4. Score the regions

    High score for regions that hang together by topic but are loosely knit inside and lean on work from outside.

  5. Test once

    The score and every threshold were frozen and pre-registered, then run a single time on the unseen 2021 to 2024 era.

Four results I threw out before one held up

Each looked like a finding. Each was killed by a control. The protocol that caught them is the most reusable part of the thesis.

  • –

    Scoring individual papers. Citation count alone explained it.

  • 1.65×

    A region score that included size. Mostly a size effect: it fell to 1.09× once size was controlled.

  • 1.73×

    A proxy version of the signals. It measured the wrong thing, a stand-in for density rather than density itself.

  • 1.97×

    A graph-learning variant. Empty clusters maxed out both halves of the score. Fixed with a validity rule declared in advance.

  • 1.63×

    The frozen score. Survived controls for citation count, size, citation volume, time leakage and field diversity.

AI Act Navigator

Ask what EU AI regulation required on any given date, and get an answer with article-level citations. Pulls the law straight from the EU's open data service, and refuses when it isn't confident.

  • Python
  • SPARQL
  • BM25
  • sentence-transformers
  • FastAPI
  • Docker
How it works
  • Chunked by article, not by length. Splitting mid-article cut a rule off from its conditions; article-aware chunks lifted recall@5 from 0.51 to 0.75.
  • The biggest gain came from removing data. Recitals were 55% of the text and took 47% of search slots, but were never the answer. Dropping them lifted recall@1 from 0.39 to 0.59.
  • Every citation is verified against the passages actually retrieved: citation precision 1.00 on the evaluated set.
  • An honest weak spot: hybrid search improved the headline numbers but hurt questions about dates (0.71 to 0.42), the capability the project exists for. It's documented as the main open problem.
  • Deployed as a 150 MB Docker image on a free tier.

Predicting which clinical trials stop early

Uses only what a trial's public ClinicalTrials.gov registration said in December 2018, and checks how it ended eight years later. Tested once on 10,970 trials the model never saw.

  • PostgreSQL
  • SQL
  • LightGBM
  • scikit-learn
How it works
  • A "time machine" design. Features come from a December 2018 database snapshot and outcomes from a 2026 snapshot, so the model only knows what was knowable at the time.
  • Split by registration year: train up to 2016, validate on 2017, test on 2018. The test set was scored exactly once.
  • Useful at the top of the list. The riskiest 10% of trials stopped early 42.9% of the time, 2.2 times the average.
  • Limits stated plainly: probabilities drift on a new year and would need recalibration before use; the ranking holds up.

eBay deal finder

A bot that scans eBay for underpriced used electronics every four hours, checks the promising ones with AI, and sends alerts to Telegram. It has run unattended on free tiers since June 2026.

  • Python
  • eBay API
  • Groq
  • Gemini Vision
  • Supabase
  • GitHub Actions
How it works
  • Cheap filters before expensive ones. Hundreds of listings are cut to a few dozen with free checks, so the rate-limited AI only sees real candidates.
  • A market price that resists junk. Each product's baseline is defended in layers: catalog-ID checks, accessory and for-parts detection, outlier trimming and blending with past runs.
  • Two AI checks: a language model reads the description for scams and broken items, and a vision model checks the photo for stock images and visible damage.
  • Building its own dataset: a tracker infers sale prices from listings that disappear, to estimate real resale values over time.

How I work

The same habits run through every project above.

  • Start with a baseline.Every result needs something simple to beat, or it doesn't mean much.
  • Split data the way it will be used.Train on the past and test on the future, never on a random shuffle of both.
  • Freeze the test before looking at it.Decide the model and the metric first, then score the test set once.
  • Look for leakage before trusting a good score.A near-perfect result is a reason to check the data, not to celebrate.
  • Report what didn't work.Rejected results and weak spots go in the write-up, next to the wins.
  • Ship something people can use.A search tool, an API, a dashboard or a bot, so the work doesn't end in a notebook.
